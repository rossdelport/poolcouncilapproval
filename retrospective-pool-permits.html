What is a retrospective pool permit?

A retrospective permit (sometimes called a "building permit for existing works") is approval for a pool or spa that was built without one. It is common when people buy a home with an older pool, or when a spa was installed without anyone realising a permit was needed.

Why it matters

  • All pools and spas in Victoria must be registered with council and have a current certificate of barrier compliance
  • Unapproved pools can cause problems when you sell your home
  • Councils can issue fines and orders for unregistered or non-compliant pools
  • Your home insurance may be affected if something goes wrong

How we fix it

  • Site inspection and review of what was built
  • Plans and engineering certification of the existing pool
  • Advice on any barrier upgrades needed (and who can do them)
  • Lodging the retrospective building permit
  • Final inspection, barrier certificate and council registration

Common questions

Will I get in trouble for an unapproved pool?

Taking action to get it approved is the best step. We work with you and the building surveyor to bring the pool into line as smoothly as possible.

Can I sell a house with an unapproved pool?

You can, but it often causes delays and price negotiations. Getting the pool approved and registered first usually makes the sale smoother.
