Pool Barrier Compliance in WA

Every pool and spa in WA needs a safe, compliant barrier, and your council will come and check it. We help you pass first time.

Council pool inspections in WA

In Western Australia, every private pool and spa that can hold more than 300 mm of water must have a barrier that stops young children getting in. This includes portable and inflatable pools. Your local council must inspect every pool barrier at least once every four years, and the owner must keep it compliant at all times. Penalties for a non-compliant barrier can be up to $5,000.

Do pool fences need a permit?

Since June 2024, most pool fences in WA no longer need their own building permit. You still need one if the fence is masonry over 0.75 m high, non-masonry over 1.8 m high, also acts as a balcony balustrade, or is in a high wind (cyclone) region.

Common barrier problems we see

  • Gates that do not self-close and self-latch every time
  • Climbable objects (pot plants, BBQs, furniture) within the 900 mm non-climbable zone
  • Dividing (boundary) fences less than 1.8 m high on the pool side
  • Gaps under or between fence panels that are too big
  • Doors or windows from the house opening straight into the pool area
  • Garden beds or retaining walls that reduce the fence height

Common questions

How often does my pool barrier need inspecting?

Your local council must inspect every pool and spa barrier at least once every four years. They will contact you to arrange a time.

What happens if my barrier fails inspection?

Council will give you a list of what needs fixing and a time to fix it, then re-inspect. We help you understand the work and get it right.
