Pool Registration and Barrier Compliance

Every pool and spa in Victoria must be registered with council and have a safe, compliant barrier. We take care of both.

Pool and spa registration in Victoria

Since 2019, every swimming pool and spa in Victoria that can hold more than 300 mm of water must be registered with the local council. New pools must be registered within 30 days of the certificate of final inspection or occupancy permit. Fines apply if a pool is not registered.

Certificate of barrier compliance

Once registered, the owner must lodge a certificate of pool and spa barrier compliance with council. This certificate is issued after a registered inspector checks the fence, gates, doors and windows around the pool. The barrier must then be re-inspected every four years.

Common barrier problems we see

  • Gates that do not self-close and self-latch every time
  • Climbable objects (pot plants, BBQs, furniture) near the fence
  • Gaps under or between fence panels that are too big
  • Doors or windows from the house opening straight into the pool area
  • Garden beds or retaining walls that reduce the fence height

What we do

  • Register your pool or spa with council
  • Arrange the barrier inspection
  • Give you a clear list of any fixes needed
  • Arrange re-inspection and lodge your certificate with council

Common questions

How often does my pool barrier need inspecting?

Every four years. Your council will tell you when your next certificate of barrier compliance is due.

What happens if my barrier fails inspection?

You get a list of what needs fixing and a time to fix it. We help you understand the work and arrange the re-inspection.
