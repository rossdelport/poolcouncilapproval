Does a spa need a permit?

Yes. In Western Australia, any spa or hot tub that can hold more than 300 mm (30 cm) of water needs a building permit and a compliant safety barrier, and it will be included in council's regular pool inspections. That includes portable and above-ground spas, swim spas and in-ground spas.

Spa safety barriers and lockable lids

Every spa needs a barrier that stops young children getting in. Depending on when the spa was installed and how it is set up, this can be a pool-style fence or, in some cases, a compliant lockable lid. We tell you exactly which option applies to your spa.

What we handle

  • Building permit for the spa and any deck or platform it sits on
  • Structural checks for decks, balconies and rooftop spas
  • Safety barrier or lid compliance
  • Lodgement with your local council

Common questions

Do portable spas need council approval?

Yes, if the spa can hold more than 30 cm of water. Portable spas still need a building permit and a compliant barrier.

Is a lockable spa cover enough?

Sometimes. It depends on when the spa was installed and the standard that applies. We assess your spa and confirm whether a lid is acceptable or a fence is needed.
