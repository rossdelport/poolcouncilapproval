When do you need a pool building permit?

In Western Australia you need a building permit before you install any pool that can hold more than 300 mm (30 cm) of water. This applies to:

  • Concrete and fibreglass in-ground pools
  • Above-ground pools
  • Plunge pools and lap pools
  • Indoor pools
  • Many relocatable and inflatable pools that hold more than 30 cm of water

The permit is issued by your local council (the permit authority). Most applications are "certified", which means a registered private building surveyor first checks the design and issues a Certificate of Design Compliance (CDC). Councils then have 10 business days to decide a certified application, compared with 25 business days for an uncertified one. That is why we always lodge certified applications.

Do you also need development approval?

Most backyard pools only need a building permit. But you may also need development (planning) approval if your property is heritage listed, in a bushfire prone area, near the coast, under a special control area, or if the pool or its decking does not meet the R-Codes for setbacks and privacy. We check this for you as part of your free quote.

FAQs

Common questions

How much does a pool permit cost in WA?

The cost depends on the pool type, your council and whether a planning permit or engineering is needed. Contact us with your address and pool details for a fixed-price quote.

Can I start digging before the permit is issued?

No. Work must not start until the building permit has been issued. Building without a permit in WA can lead to fines and orders to stop work or remove it.

Who issues a pool building permit?

Your local council issues the building permit. For a certified application, a private building surveyor first issues a Certificate of Design Compliance. We manage this whole process for you.
