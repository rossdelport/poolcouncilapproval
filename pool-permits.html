When do you need a pool building permit?

In Victoria you need a building permit before you install any pool that can hold more than 300 mm (30 cm) of water. This applies to:

  • Concrete and fibreglass in-ground pools
  • Above-ground pools
  • Plunge pools and lap pools
  • Indoor pools
  • Many relocatable and inflatable pools that hold more than 30 cm of water

The permit is issued by a registered building surveyor. It checks that your pool is structurally sound, sits correctly on your block, and has a compliant child safety barrier.

Do you also need a planning permit?

Most backyard pools only need a building permit. But you may also need a planning permit if your property is affected by a heritage overlay, vegetation or flood overlay, bushfire controls, or if the pool is close to a boundary or easement. We check this for you as part of your free quote.

Common questions

How much does a pool permit cost in Victoria?

The cost depends on the pool type, your council and whether a planning permit or engineering is needed. Contact us with your address and pool details for a fixed-price quote.

Can I start digging before the permit is issued?

No. Work must not start until the building permit has been issued. Starting early can lead to fines and stop-work orders.

Who issues a pool building permit?

A registered building surveyor, either a private surveyor or the council's municipal building surveyor. We manage this process for you.
